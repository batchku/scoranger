Stance

The signposted option. Every screen has the same three fixed bands: a ribbon at the top that names where you are as a trail (Library › Tuesday at the Ship › People), a chip strip under it that holds every choice that applies to what is on screen or selected, and a dock at the bottom for the transport. Between them, the content area is the only thing that changes, and it changes in place with a short cross-fade, never a slide-down and never a shove. There are no dropdowns: Sort's four choices are four chips that are simply there; a selected row's actions appear in the strip, where the last selection's were. The trail is the way back, one tap per level. Forms are friendly but plain: 8pt corners, hairlines kept but softened to the lightest line, big Space Grotesk in the ribbon, and the numeral as a small clay square in the trail, the way a hall numbers its doors.

Disclosure pattern, in one sentence: a destination replaces the content area in place under a ribbon that names where you are; choices live in a fixed chip strip under the ribbon, so nothing ever drops down or pushes.

Transport and mixer share the dock: the transport is its 60pt face, always present in the score view; the mixer is a fixed 120pt row that opens above it, once, and the score shortens once.

Performance mode folds the ribbon and the dock to two 4pt clay lines at the top and bottom edges. Tap either to unfold.

Grid and centring

What it costs to build

  • Changes shape: a Ribbon and a Strip replace every screen's nav bar, control bar, band header and inline reveal; the ☰ screens become strip chips over swappable content; sort and filter become chips and lose their reveals entirely; the selection model gains a selected-row state that feeds the strip. The dock absorbs the transport bar, the mixer window (drag, park and clamp go) and the thumbnail strip. The route model is unchanged, the ribbon reads it.
  • Tokens only: rCtl 2 to 8, rPanel 3 to 12, line2 to line, PanelToggle to a capsule, the numeral to the square in the trail, the trail's 16pt role.
  • Unchanged: the score canvas and gestures, the knob, the system pickers, push navigation as the model underneath, the identifiers.
  • Risks: the strip's width is finite: nine settings chips fit an iPad and scroll on a phone, and at AX text sizes the strip becomes a vertical list by IPHONE_0.6.14 §6.3 rule 4, which reintroduces height. The ribbon costs 56pt of every screen including the score; performance mode gives it back.
